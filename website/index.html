MADE FOR THE MOMENT

A focused start.
Getting better in the open.

Open-Q is an actively developed project. These are the tools available today, with more polish and capabilities to come.

01

Analogue-matched responses

Ivantsov-based filter designs target analogue magnitude responses. See how our bell curves compare with the reference as frequency approaches Nyquist.

Compare the filter curves →
02

24 bands. Five filter types.

Bell, low/high shelf and low/high cut filters. Choose cut slopes from 6 to 96 dB/octave, including 18 dB/octave.

03

Shape directly on the graph

Drag individually coloured nodes and fine-tune frequency, gain and Q in floating band controls. Resize the interface to fit your workspace.

04

Pre/post spectrum analysis

Compare input and output spectra, with Normal/High quality, ERB or octave smoothing, and adjustable decay.

05

Gain, meters and bypass

Input/output gain controls and peak meters keep levels in view. Audition changes with per-band bypass and smooth global bypass.

06

Presets and DAW recall

Organize presets in groups, find them with search, automate parameters in your DAW and recall settings with your session.

UNDER THE CURVE

Explore the filter response.

Compare Open-Q’s Ivantsov-based bell, shelf and cut responses with an analogue reference, JUCE and conventional prewarped BLT. Both frequency-axis styles show the same steady-state responses, without oversampling.

Bell · 48 kHz · Q = 1 · ±12 dB · centres at 1.2, 12 and 19.2 kHz. Each curve represents an independent band, not their combined response. Error panels are stacked by centre; solid error lines show boosts and dashed lines show cuts. JUCE and BLT share an error trace because they overlap.

Download the Bell response data (CSV) or reproduce the plots and download either axis style. Method: RBJ analogue bell / BLT conventions; comparison presentation inspired by Laurent de Soras.
